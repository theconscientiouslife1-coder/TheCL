The Science of Our Diet

****This blog will only repeat scientific information that has been cited by creditable institutions.****

Never in the history of civilisation are we so conscious of not only the importance of a good diet but also the detrimental effects of a poor diet1. The science of food has advanced past just the understanding of the nutrients that can be attained from foods but we have a clearer picture of what food compositions have on the body both physically and psychologically.

However, with information so accessible and advice been easily posted, there is significant information which is very much in the pseudoscience bucket. This leads to misinformation which subsequently contradicts what is and isn’t good for someone’s WELL-BEING2.

One of the understandings of our diet is how it is impacting our gut3. These bacteria produce compounds; short chain fatty acids (SCFA), which are vital for gut health, strengthening the intestinal barrier, and reducing inflammation.

From a dietary perspective, an unhealthy gut arises primarily from processed and high fat foods. To counter this one of the essential SCFA is Butyrate, which is produced by gut bacteria fermenting dietary fibre in the colon. The benefits of Butyrate are that it possesses anti-inflammatory properties and helps regulate the immune system.

Which brings us to the GUT-BRAIN AXIS. This is where the gut and the brain are in constant communication with one another via the vagus nerve. This connection influences digestion, mood, immunity, and stress responses. The gut is covered in nerves lining the digestive tract which is named the enteric nervous system (ENS).

When the Gut-Brain Axis is in-sync we are more balanced and are in a higher state allowing us to take advantage of the many positives which come from being clear in body and mind.
