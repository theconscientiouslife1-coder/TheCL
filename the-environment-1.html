The Environment I

We live in a world where we have become sanitised and at worse apathetic by all we see and hear. TV screens, personal devices are constantly churning out the ills of the world, nevertheless, if we are not experiencing the catastrophises for ourselves, we simply scroll past and tune out.

The environment crisis is no different, however where we have had wars, famines, economic collapses and various other crisis throughout the millennia, humanity has never experienced the degradation of its environment in those moments. We on the other hand are dealing with such a situation. The space where we experiment with the ideas we have within, engage with others, work, enjoy, rest is now in a slow decline and the world that will remain will be nothing to what any civilisation has encountered.

How did we get here? Well one key component is the economic experiment, which began 50 years ago, [neo-liberalism]; it denotes a conception of freedom as an overarching social value associated with reducing state functions to those of a minimal state. The over-arching aspect of the individual, in this ideology, is the freedom to consume, which basically reduces us to the level of consumers.

Of the various economic schools that endorsed neo-liberalism, there was an argument against planned societies i.e. communism, that it is impossible to build such societies as individuals only have a limited amount of knowledge on how the world operates therefore to attempt to plan would not provide a universal picture as to what a society should be. In addition, it was also argued that the very essence of being human is the belief that we are autonomous i.e. free to make our own choices.

The rationale that we only have a limited knowledge of the world has unfortunately led to the situation where individuals are unaware of the impacts their consumption habits lead to, neo-liberalism is almost excusing us for not thinking beyond those actions has on the environment. But the consequences of our actions have led us to where we are at, if not even past, the breaking point of the eco-system that sustains life on this planet.
