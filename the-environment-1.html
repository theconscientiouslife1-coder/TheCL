The Environment I

We live in a world where we have become sanitised and at worse apathetic by all we see and hear. TV screens, personal devices are constantly churning out the ills of the world, nevertheless, if we are not experiencing the catastrophises for ourselves, we simply scroll past and tune out.

The environment crisis is no different, however where we have had wars, famines, economic collapses and various other crisis throughout the millennia, humanity has never experienced the degradation of its environment in those moments. We on the other hand are dealing with such a situation. The space where we experiment with the ideas we have within, engage with others, work, enjoy, rest is now in a slow decline and the world that will remain will be nothing to what any civilisation has encountered.

How did we get here? Well one key component is the economic experiment, which began 50 years ago, neo-liberalism; it denotes a conception of freedom as an overarching social value associated with reducing state functions to those of a minimal state. The over-arching aspect of the individual, in this ideology, is the freedom to consume, which basically reduces us to the level of consumers.

Of the various economic schools that endorsed neo-liberalism, there was an argument against planned societies i.e. communism, that it is impossible to build such societies as individuals only have a limited amount of knowledge on how the world operates therefore to attempt to plan would not provide a universal picture as to what a society should be. In addition, it was also argued that the very essence of being human is the belief that we are autonomous i.e. free to make our own choices.

The rationale that we only have a limited knowledge of the world has unfortunately led to the situation where individuals are unaware of the impacts their consumption habits lead to, neo-liberalism is almost excusing us for not thinking beyond those actions has on the environment. But the consequences of our actions have led us to where we are at, if not even past, the breaking point of the eco-system that sustains life on this planet.

Unfettered consumption is a total contradiction in a finite world. For over four decades we have seen consumption on a scale never witnessed before. From figure 1. below, energy consumption has more than doubled in that period. And from a consumer perspective this has increased by, some estimates, over four times that of the 1970’s, whereas population growth has only doubled.

Figure 1. Global primary energy consumption by source

The European Commission predicts that between now and 2030, there will be an additional 1.3 billion more consumers than there are today. These numbers will be from the “Global South” where living standards are currently not on a par with Western countries, and no one is denying that they should have the same basics as those in the “Global North”. However, consider the current impact on the environment, what will be the consequences of 1.3 billion more consumers have?

This is not an alarmist post on the state of the environment, but this slow decent into the destruction of the multi facets of the environment, requires action across the various stakeholders in society and it all begins at a personal level. Economics is very simple, if there is demand, then supply will follow. Therefore, if we continue to purchase items, that we know have a negative impact on the environment then there is no solution to this crisis.

One of the great travesties that has come out of the current economic model is that products are in abundance. Spend hours on an ecommerce site like Amazon, where products are so cheap, that the value of everything has been diminished. This has led to the psychological state whereby one can purchase, have little regard for the product, if breaks I can just reorder. The Conscientious Life requires you to make informed decisions and think beyond the immediate.

An example: when we buy something, this is where we need to be in the NOW, we must understand where did it come from, if plastic can it be recycled or better still is there a similar product with less waste material. An old adage was Reduce, Reuse, Recycle in that order. This is not easy and it can feel like the ship is already going down but to display what it is that makes us human i.e. we are not consumers, the products when we purchase do not define us but when we do buy, we can make informed autonomous decisions which re-enforces our essence. This has the ability of re-enforcing our BEING when we act in such a manner. Taking consideration to both our own needs, future needs and the environment.
