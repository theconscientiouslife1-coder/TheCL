The Conscientious Life (Part 1)

What does it mean to be Conscientious? The Oxford Reference Dictionary defines it as; showing or done with careful attention. Conscientiousness is a word that has at its basis of one being careful before acting. It is one of the Big Five personality traits. The opposite would be behaving rashly, no consideration in how one behaves or the consequences of such behaviour.

Conscientiousness is mainly synonymous in the modern world with reference to a person’s work ethic. From this it conjures up notions of one who is diligent, attentive, persistent, careful, thoughtful, efficient, organised. But it also much more than this; it refers to completing obligations to others in a serious manner; one may feel the need to cut corners to get the job done in order to say that it was completed, but in this context, conscientiousness considers all aspects and strives to ensure all elements that are associated to the work are considered at the very least. Hence, there is an element of duty and that is one of the defining aspects of the Conscientious Life.

Our freedom is predicated on the fact we have reason, which distinguishes us in the animal kingdom, and this allows us to consciously consider what is in front of us which gives us the ability of being able to act in accordance with how we have weighed up the scenario we are confronted with, we are autonomous. Of course, in this post-modern world, it is our individual desires that are placed as paramount and reason takes a back seat. We are more inclined to satisfy the "Will" than to actually take the time to reason the scenario out. This lack thereof can lead to consequences that have adverse effects. The environment is one such example.

How often do we give any consideration for what we take from the environment or how we act within it. We live isolated lives at times, atomised existences not knowing or being conscious of what the effects of our actions have. Immanual Kant spoke of a Kingdom of Ends which implied that one acts toward others and/or the environment as an end themselves and not just a means to some subjective end.

We are all trying to get by and with those closest to us in the most efficient manner possible. And for this we are all in the same boat. But for better or worse the historical dialect of humanity has led us to the final end of humanity as an isolated individual where its self-interested nature is paramount in its interactions with its world (commercialisation of society). This very much aligns itself with attaining as much as possible with little regard for everything else. Neo-Liberal economics plays on the limited knowledge any one can have. Furthermore, we can also look upon it in regard to the isolated individual who can never understand the full sequence of how objects arise or are fully dispensed with. But this mode of understanding is not justified or acceptable anymore. The first step is lifting the veil and beginning to see the world as it really is, not how it is being portrayed to us, both from the subjective and objective mode that we are compounded with in our daily lives.
