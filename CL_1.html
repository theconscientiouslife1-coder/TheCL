Conscientiousness

The Conscientious Life (Part 1)

What does it mean to be Conscientious? The Oxford Reference Dictionary defines it: "showing or done with careful attention". At its basis, it is the act of being careful before acting.

Conscientiousness is one of the "Big Five" personality traits. The opposite would be behaving rashly, with no consideration for how one behaves or the consequences of such behavior. While it is often synonymous in the modern world with reference to a person’s work—conjuring notions of diligence and efficiency—it is much more than that. It refers to completing obligations to others in a serious manner.

"There is an element of duty, and that is one of the defining aspects of the Conscientious Life."

Our freedom is predicated on the fact that we have reason. This allows us to consciously consider what is in front of us, giving us the ability to act in accordance with how we have weighed up the scenario. However, in our post-modern world, individual desires are placed as paramount, which has led to environmental degradation and an atomized existence where we are unaware of the effects of our actions.

Immanual Kant spoke of a Kingdom of Ends, which implies that one acts toward others and the environment as an end in themselves, and not just a means to some subjective end. But the historical dialect has led us to the "final end" of humanity as isolated individuals where self-interest is paramount.

This mode of understanding is no longer justified. We must choose: either remain oblivious while the world as we know it ceases to be, or become active citizens who deliberately make decisions where actions are not just for the "I".
