Consumption and the Breaking Point
We live in a world where we have become sanitised and at worse apathetic by all we see and hear. TV screens and personal devices are constantly churning out the ills of the world, yet unless we experience the catastrophes ourselves, we simply scroll past.

We are almost 50 years into the economic experiment of neo-liberalism, which sought to allow the individual the freedom to consume as much as they wanted. For over four decades, we have witnessed consumption on a scale never witnessed before. This rationale was built on the premise that planned societies are impossible; unfortunately, this has led to a situation where individuals are unaware of the effects their consumption has on the world.

"Unfettered consumption is in contradiction to a finite world. We have reached the environmental breaking point."

The modern economy works on the basis of all our individual desires being fulfilled, followed by new enticements to propel the ship forward. As Schopenhauer observed, once we have attained a desire, we move on to the next. This lust for objects is often anticlimactic; the feeling of attainment rarely meets our expectations.

Yet, there is a debt to be paid for these desires. It is the environment that pays the heavy price—from raw materials to manufacturing and disposal. Our over-consumption depletes the earth.
