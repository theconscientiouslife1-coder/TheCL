Consumption, Conscientiousness, and the Breaking Point

By Your Name • January 20, 2026

We live in a world where we have become sanitised and at worse apathetic by all we see and hear. While our personal devices churn out the ills of the world, we often scroll past unless we experience catastrophes for ourselves.

We are almost 50 years into the economic experiment of neo-liberalism, which sought to allow the individual the freedom to consume as much as they wanted. For over four decades, we have witnessed consumption on a scale never witnessed before. This rationale built on the premise that it is impossible to plan societies because individuals have limited knowledge. Unfortunately, this has led to a situation where individuals are unaware of—and effectively excused from—the consequences of their consumption.

"Unfettered consumption is in contradiction to a finite world. We have reached the environmental breaking point."

The Cycle of Desire

The modern economy functions by fulfilling all our individual desires and then bringing more about to entice us, propelling this ship forward. As Schopenhauer observed, once we have attained a desire, we move on to the next. This lust for objects is often anticlimactic; the feeling of attainment rarely meets our expectations.

There is a heavy debt for these desires, paid by the environment through materials, manufacture, and disposal. Our over-consumption depletes the earth, and when our goods are disposed of, they are not organically broken down.

Conclusion: A Necessary Transition

This slow descent into destruction requires action from all stakeholders, starting at the personal level. Protecting the mainstays of life—food and water—is fundamentally more important than unfettered consumer freedom. Transitioning to a conscientious life will require sacrifices in terms of current liberties and economic livelihoods, but the alternative is no longer tenable.
