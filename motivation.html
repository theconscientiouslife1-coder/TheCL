Motivation

Attaining your goals requires being physically fit, which provides the necessary propulsion to reach your personal aspirations[cite: 32, 33]. The key to getting into a fitness rhythm is ensuring your goals are realistic—you are twice as likely to continue if you don't try to achieve too much too soon[cite: 34, 35].

Action leads to the firing of neurons, creating a virtuous circle of endorphins that reinforces the brain to remember the positive effect[cite: 36]. When this pattern is embedded into daily life, we enter the realm of HABIT[cite: 38].

The hardest part is the start—moving from static to motion[cite: 39, 40]. We must understand the “3 Ms”: Motivation, Motion, and Momentum[cite: 43]. Once you have momentum, don't overthink it—just do it![cite: 44]. Avoid self-loathing by being realistic in the short-term; eat the elephant one little piece at a time[cite: 46, 49].
