Motivation

We all want to be the best version of ourselves and this is no bad thing. Goals, aspirations are what makes us who we are. A key aspect in attaining those goals successfully is being physically fit. This is not something that can be switched on/off any time, it is a frequent ask on us to find the time to exert energy which will prove the necessary propulsion to achieving our personal goals.

The key to getting into a fitness rhythm is to be sure to only attempt achieving fitness goals that are realistic. If your fitness programme is realistic then you are twice as likely to continue than if you try to achieve too much too soon. Like all positives in life once we enact an action this leads to the firing of neurons which is a virtuous circle releasing endorphin which cause one’s mood to become positive thus re-enforcing the action which further leads to the brain remembering the great effect from the cause. This one action then further motivates one to regularly want to exercise.

If this pattern can be embedded into one’s daily activities, then we begin to enter the realms of HABIT. The hardest part of any action is to start. Standing still, is the perspective of us when we are viewing an activity where we need to get into motion. This is a binary scenario – from static to motion. To move we need to factor in the potential rewards/possibilities that our excursions will achieve to get the ball to start rolling. No matter how little that does move momentum has begun. This is where we understand the “3 Ms” – Motivation, Motion, Momentum. Each a stepping stone and when you have momentum it’s a case of just doing it, don’t over think it!

One of the worse things we can do is to intend to carry out some task and then after the lapsed period, of how long it would have taken to carry out the activity; fitness or other, we would be finished and feeling pretty happy with ourself, rather than sitting in self-loathing for not carry out our intention. This brings us back to being realistic in what is achievable in the short-term. If our goals are too daunting at the outset, we are likely to consider the effort too much. But we must always remember, especially at the outset of attempting something new, we cannot achieve everything in one go, it is a piecemeal exercise, eat the elephant one little piece at a time.
