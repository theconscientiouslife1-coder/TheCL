Fitness | Fundamental Number 2

This blog makes reference to 3 fundamental elements that are the foundation for the potential one has in life, to allow one to excel in whatever one wants to do. The second fundamental is fitness; a key foundational stone not just for living well but also driving new ideas/goals and the focus to achieve those aspirations. In this category we will discuss the importance of fitness to one’s well-being, the various aspects of fitness & how to build it in to one’s routine along with tips and hints.

We are bombarded about the benefits of exercise, from completing 10,000 steps a day to having a brisk walk for 20 minutes. This blog will focus on the benefits of applying intense exercise activities on a regular basis. We will not discount the importance of those who are not exercising and will provide steps etc. that encourages such activity. The multiple factors as to why one should execute an intense fitness regime; utilising one’s time in an efficient manner, burning more calories and build muscle in a shorter period (must be stressed that this level of exercise should only be undertaken once a person’s level of fitness is at a satisfactory level).

Also, and from the perspective of the Conscientious Life, Improved Heart Health and Aerobic Capacity: Pushing your cardiovascular system with intense exercise strengthens your heart and lungs, improves blood pressure and cholesterol levels, and increases your aerobic capacity (VO2 max) more effectively than moderate activity alone. This allows more blood to be circulated around the physical body. From the benefits above, especially the last element, this feeds into the importance of fitness in one’s life is to not just make us feel good about ourselves but the benefits to both our physical and psychological states.

Studies on improving our memory suggest exercise is a fundamental counter as we age. In addition, exercise improves the increase of the protein brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) in the bloodstream. This protein is essential to developing new neurons, called neurogenesis, which is key to learning and memory. These new cells are vital for maintaining cognitive flexibility, forming new memories, and adapting to new experiences. Since the hippocampus is critical for memory consolidation and learning, the generation of new neurons and increased plasticity in this brain region may explain the improved cognition and emotional state that accompanies exercise.
